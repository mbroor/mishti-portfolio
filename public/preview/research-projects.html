01 / Liu-CoMotion Lab, UIC

EEG and neurorehabilitation research

Conventional EEG electrodes can struggle to reach the scalp through dense Afro textured hairstyles. I designed and tested novel attachments for Type IV hair, combining CAD and resin printing with human subject measurements to evaluate scalp contact, signal quality, and comfort.

DesignOpen Hair and Braided Hair attachment geometries
TestingImpedance, electrode disconnections, scalp distance, alpha SNR and comfort
ApplicationMore accessible EEG acquisition across hair types and movement tasks
Technical highlights +
  • Modeled and resin printed attachments to accommodate different hairstyles while improving electrode contact.
  • Worked with 32 channel EEG, IMUs and instrumented insoles in multimodal gait and mobility research.
  • Conducted impedance, signal preprocessing, electrode distance and participant comfort assessments.
  • The study poster reports fewer disconnections, lower electrode impedance and shorter electrode to scalp distance for the novel system. Resting alpha signal to noise ratio was comparable to the conventional cap; the comfort comparison did not reach conventional statistical significance.

02 / UIC Senior Design

Deformable 3D in vitro spinal subarachnoid model

Our senior design team developed an anatomical model of the spinal subarachnoid space to study cerebrospinal fluid flow and inform future intrathecal drug delivery research. The design combines an optically clear rigid cervical component with a flexible silicone dura mimic.

FabricationFusion 360 molds, water soluble PVA printing and silicone casting
Prototype result10 cm hybrid assembly demonstrating a sealed resin and silicone interface
ModelingConservation based CSF transport simulations and MATLAB optical analysis
Technical highlights +
  • Designed negative molds in Fusion 360, printed PVA scaffolds, cast silicone and dissolved the sacrificial structures to create a deformable channel.
  • Developed a clear resin cervical segment for higher resolution visualization of anatomical details.
  • The team modeled CSF motion and tracer dispersion using conservation based equations and evaluated optical transparency with MATLAB image analysis.
  • The presented 10 cm hybrid prototype validated material bonding and sealing. A full scale 60 cm model, pressure sensing and further physiological verification were described as ongoing or future work.

Senior design team project. First place in Biomedical Engineering and fourth overall at the 2026 UIC Engineering Expo.

03 / Independent research and development

EEG motor imagery classification

I built a reproducible machine learning pipeline to distinguish imagined left and right hand movement using 64 channel EEG data from the PhysioNet Motor Movement and Imagery dataset, recorded using BCI2000. Rather than stop at classification accuracy, I assessed variability across participants and possible nonmotor signal contributions.

69%Mean cross validation accuracy for a single subject using CSP
5 of 30Subjects with significant above chance performance in binomial tests
96%Subject 7 motor region only channel control, not a population result
Technical highlights +
  • Loaded and preprocessed EEG in MNE Python, applied 8 to 30 Hz filtering, segmented trials and trained scikit learn classifiers.
  • Compared naive per channel features with Common Spatial Patterns. Mean accuracy increased from near chance to 69% in a single subject analysis.
  • Evaluated all 30 participants individually. Five met the uncorrected p less than 0.05 binomial threshold; the average across all subjects was 53%, and the group test was not significant.
  • Compared full channel decoding with a central sensorimotor electrode subset for subject 7. Preserved accuracy suggests a motor related contribution, though channel restriction alone cannot establish the exact neural source or eliminate every confound.
  • Documented the pipeline, accuracy distribution and CSP scalp patterns in the public repository.
