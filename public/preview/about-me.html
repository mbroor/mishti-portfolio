About Me

I'm a biomedical engineer interested in the details that make neural-sensing technology usable outside the lab.

I started working with EEG hardware during my undergraduate research at UIC. Much of that work involved practical questions: how do you keep an electrode in reliable contact with the scalp, especially across different hair types and styles? How do you know whether a design is actually helping?

I worked on CAD, 3D-printed attachments, participant testing, and signal analysis. I liked moving between the design and the experiment. Finding out which assumptions held up once we tried something.

I'm now pursuing a thesis-track M.S. in Biomedical Engineering at Northwestern. My research involves a wearable clinical EEG platform, and I'm continuing to learn more about bioelectronics and medical-device development.

My approach

Practical questions matter.

Good signals depend on more than software. The interface between the device and the person matters. That includes electrode contact, comfort, and how a prototype holds up during testing.
